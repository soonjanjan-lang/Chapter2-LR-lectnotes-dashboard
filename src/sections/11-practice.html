Tutorials

Fictional studies are marked †. Each tutorial takes 15 to 30 minutes.

T1. Build and test a Boolean search string

Research question: Does participation in microfinance increase the business income of women entrepreneurs in Southeast Asia?

  1. Build a concept table with three or four concepts and at least three synonyms each.
  2. Write one Google Scholar string and one Scopus string.
  3. Run the Google Scholar string. Record the number of hits, and judge how many of the first 20 results are relevant.
  4. Refine the string once and record the change.

You can use the Boolean query builder.

Common refinement: the setting is left out at first, because the strongest evidence on microcredit comes from randomised evaluations in several countries, including outside Asia; Southeast Asian studies are then found with a second search that adds the country names.

T2. Rewrite a laundry list as a synthesis

Rewrite this paragraph as one synthesis paragraph using the four moves (claim, evidence, analysis, link).

Aziz (2014)† studied the minimum wage in Malaysia. He used data from 2010 to 2013 and found that employment fell in manufacturing. Chong (2016)† studied the minimum wage. She found no effect on employment in services using Labour Force Survey data. Devi and Tan (2018)† also studied the minimum wage and found that wages rose for low-paid workers but employment did not change. Ismail (2019)† found that small firms reduced hiring after the minimum wage.

Reveal model answer

Malaysian evidence on the employment effect of the 2013 minimum wage is mixed, and the pattern of results suggests the effect depends on sector and firm size. Studies of services and of the low-paid workforce as a whole find that wages rose without measurable job losses (Chong, 2016†; Devi & Tan, 2018†), whereas studies of manufacturing and of small firms report falling employment or slower hiring (Aziz, 2014†; Ismail, 2019†). All four studies compare periods before and after 2013 without a control group, so none can separate the effect of the policy from the economic slowdown of 2015 and 2016. Whether the minimum wage reduced employment in sectors where it bound most tightly therefore remains an open question, which this study addresses by exploiting differences in the minimum wage bite across districts.

What changed: one claim opens the paragraph; studies are grouped by finding; a shared weakness is identified; the last sentence links to the new study.

T3. Build a matrix from three abstracts

Fill a matrix with these columns: author (year), country and period, data and N, method, main finding, limitation. Then write one sentence on what the three studies have in common.

Abstract 1. Hamzah (2020)† uses the 2016 Household Expenditure Survey (N = 14,500) to estimate Engel curves for electricity spending in Malaysia. The electricity budget share falls with income; households in the lowest quintile spend 4.1% of their budget on electricity, compared with 1.8% in the highest.

Abstract 2. Nguyen and Lee (2021)† study the removal of an electricity subsidy in Vietnam in 2016 using a panel of 2,300 households observed in 2014 and 2018. Difference-in-differences estimates show that consumption by poorer households fell by 9% relative to richer households.

Abstract 3. Salleh (2022)† surveys 400 households in Kedah and finds, using a logit model, that awareness of energy-efficiency labels raises the probability of buying an efficient appliance. The author notes that the sample is not representative.

T4. Identify gap types

Take the eight-item quiz in the Literature gap lesson. Then answer: which type, or which two types, describes the gap in your own study? Write one sentence explaining why the gap matters.

Reveal guidance

Most economics theses combine a substantive gap (population, contextual, temporal or empirical) with a methodological one. Name the main type first. If your sentence on why the gap matters could be pasted into any thesis ("it will contribute to the literature"), it is not yet specific enough: say what decision or theory would change.

T6. Is this invitation genuine?

Read the email and list every warning sign. Then complete the case cards in the journal quality lesson.

Dear Esteemed Professor/Doctor,

Greetings from the Global Journal of Advanced Economics, Business, Engineering and Social Studies†! We have read your excellent paper and invite you to submit your next manuscript. Our journal is indexed in Google Scholar, Index Copernicus and International Scientific Indexing (ISI), with Impact Factor 7.95 (2025). Peer review is completed within 72 hours and publication within one week. Publication fee: USD 99 only, payable after acceptance.

Kind regards, Editorial Office (globaljournal.editor@gmail.com)

Reveal model answer
  • Generic, flattering greeting; no name, no specific paper.
  • Absurdly broad scope (economics to engineering).
  • "Indexed in Google Scholar" offered as a quality signal; it is not one.
  • "International Scientific Indexing (ISI)" imitates the old name of Clarivate's Institute for Scientific Information.
  • An "impact factor" not issued by Clarivate.
  • Review in 72 hours: real peer review takes weeks or months.
  • Fee revealed in a spam email; free webmail address for the editorial office.
T7. Spot and remove the slop

For each passage, list the tells, then rewrite it plainly. Check your diagnosis with the slop scanner.

Passage 1. "In the ever-evolving landscape of higher education, graduate employability plays a pivotal role. This multifaceted issue has been delved into by numerous scholars, shedding light on its intricate dynamics."

Passage 2. "Moreover, remittances not only empower households but also foster resilience, sustainability, and growth. Furthermore, studies have shown that remittances are crucial."

Passage 3. "Overall, this underscores the importance of a holistic approach to poverty — one that is comprehensive, inclusive, and transformative."

Reveal model answers

Passage 1. Tells: generic opener, "plays a pivotal role", "multifaceted", "delved into", "shedding light on", "intricate dynamics", no citations. Rewrite: "Graduate unemployment in Malaysia has been studied mainly through tracer-study data, which record whether graduates are employed six months after graduation but not whether their jobs match their qualifications."

Passage 2. Tells: sentence-initial connectors, "not only ... but also", a list of three abstract nouns, "studies have shown" with no citation, "crucial". Rewrite: "In panel data from rural Uganda, households that used mobile money received larger and more frequent remittances and had higher consumption (Munyegera & Matsumoto, 2016)."

Passage 3. Tells: "Overall", "underscores the importance of", "holistic", em dash, list of three adjectives, no content. Rewrite: delete it, or replace it with the specific implication: "Cash transfers would therefore reach more of the households affected than price controls on rice alone."

T8. Write a gap statement

Scenario. Your matrix holds 26 studies on household saving. Twenty use data on salaried employees. Theory predicts that income volatility increases precautionary saving, but liquidity constraints reduce the ability to save. Malaysia's gig workforce has grown, and most gig workers are not required to contribute to the EPF. You have access to a 2025 survey of 900 e-hailing and delivery workers.

Write a gap statement using the four moves: known, missing, why it matters, what this study does.

Reveal model answer

Although the determinants of household saving are well documented, the evidence comes almost entirely from salaried employees whose saving is partly compulsory through EPF contributions. Gig workers differ in two ways that theory predicts will matter, and in opposite directions: their income is more volatile, which should raise precautionary saving, and they lack automatic contributions and often face liquidity constraints, which should reduce it. How these forces balance is unknown. The answer matters because the share of workers outside the compulsory pension system is growing, with implications for old-age poverty. Using a 2025 survey of 900 e-hailing and delivery workers, this study estimates how income volatility and liquidity constraints affect their saving.
